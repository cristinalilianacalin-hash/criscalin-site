Human leadership in an AI world

I'm Cris. Better than yesterday.

Short, honest ideas about people at work. Bosses, teams, fear, praise, the meeting that still bothers you at 2 a.m. Twenty years of leading people across Europe, the US, India and Asia taught me this: the hard part is rarely the technology.

Why I do this

I believe there is light in every person. Sometimes it is well hidden. Behind a deadline, a bad Monday, or a manager who has not said “good job” in six months.

Most leaders I have met were never taught how to lead. They were very good at the work, so one day someone gave them people. Nobody told them the job had changed. Nobody told them that a big part of what they are now paid for is to see people, and to say it out loud. We all need that. Executives too. Especially on Monday morning.

I also believe that fear is not power. It only looks like power from far away. Up close, it is frustration wearing a nice jacket.

And no, I don’t think AI will take our jobs. I think it will make one question louder: what do we bring that a machine can’t? My answer is meaning. The reason behind the numbers. The person behind the task. People are not hired only to deliver.

I know this one from the inside. The first time I became a manager, nobody trained me. The organisation gave me a team and assumed I would figure it out. So I did what most of us do: I copied what I had seen, good and bad, and learned the rest the hard way. Then I started reading. Psychology, leadership, studies, one book after another. I am still reading.

Somewhere along the way I understood how big the need is. Not for more theory. For plain, natural guidance, the kind a good friend gives you before a hard meeting. And I believe every one of us has a moral duty to give back something of what we learned, to people and to life.

So this is what I give back. Short pieces of documented knowledge from books, studies and twenty years of leading people, the good parts and the painful ones. A few tools you can use tomorrow. I’m not a psychologist and I’m not a guru. I read a lot, I have made plenty of mistakes, and I still believe in people.

For close to twenty years I have worked inside large projects and large teams, at times around 200 people. How did I make it work? By looking at the human patterns behind the job titles. I have led people from Romania to the US, from India to Japan, and I learned the same thing everywhere: the title changes, the human being behind it does not. In the AI era this matters even more. Machines can take the routine; understanding and accepting the person next to you, even when they are very different from you, stays our job.

I’m not trying to be perfect. Just better than yesterday.

Mean-tering™

My method has a small joke inside. The word “mean” has three meanings, and each one is a job for a leader.

mean, as in unkind
Take the meanness out of work, in what people do and in what people feel. And always ask what is behind it.
mean, as in meaning
We deliver numbers. But what are we really delivering through those numbers?
mean, as in “what does this mean?”
A method asks for a document, a meeting, a template. What is the logic behind it? The name of the method matters less than the logic.
